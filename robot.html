Side Project · Undergraduate Capstone, 2021

Autonomous Water-Cleaning Robot for Low-Clearance Spaces

In fertilizer plants and rice mills, sludge builds up in spaces whose openings are too low for a person to get through. This tracked robot drives into such a space, maps it with a 2D LiDAR, explores it on its own and washes the floor with a water spray. Tracks instead of wheels keep it from slipping on wet sludge.

Overview

Our two-person team built this robot from scratch. We designed the tracked aluminium frame in CAD and machined it on a CNC, then put the battery, a Jetson Nano, the motor driver and a solenoid-valve water spray onto it. On the Jetson we set up ROS so the robot maps the space with a 2D LiDAR, explores it on its own and drives its motors over serial. The spray valve is switched through the motor driver's I/O, so it opens while the robot drives and closes as soon as it stops.

Hardware

An aluminium-profile frame with CNC-machined mounts carries two DC-motor-driven tracks, a waterproof electronics box and an RPLIDAR A3 on top.

ROS software

A SLAM module builds a map from the LiDAR scans and tracks the robot’s position on it. Our path planner picks where to go next, and our motor interface turns the resulting velocity commands into motor commands while opening the spray valve only when the robot moves.
